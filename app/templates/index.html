FAQ

Questions, answered honestly

The boring-but-true details — cost, accounts, AI, and why your city matters more than any generic list.

Is Hamara Bagh really free?

Yes — free and open source, with no ads and no paid tier. No signup, no app to install, no newsletter pop-ups. Type your city and start growing.

Do I need an account to use the journal?

No. Your journal, watering reminders and photos are tied to a private ID stored in your own browser — no email, no password. The trade-off: clearing your browser data clears the journal, so keep it on the device you garden with.

Does it work outside Pakistan?

The recommendation engine works for any city worldwide — it geocodes your location and pulls a live forecast. The hand-written layer is Pakistan-first though: 115 city guides, Rabi/Kharif timing, smog and frost notes, and Urdu touches throughout.

What are Rabi and Kharif?

Pakistan's two growing seasons. Rabi is the cool season — sown roughly October to March: wheat, peas, spinach, coriander, most salad greens. Kharif is the hot season with the monsoon — April to September: okra, chillies, melons, maize, most flowering annuals. Planting in the wrong season is the single most common reason seeds fail. See the sowing calendar →

I searched "what to grow in Karachi in October" — will this help?

That's exactly the question this site was built around. Instead of a generic list written for another country, Hamara Bagh checks your city's live forecast, the current season and your space, then ranks plants 0–100 with a plain-English reason for every score.

What about the AI plant doctor?

The symptom checker is a rule-based diagnosis engine — instant, private and available with zero AI. When a free Hugging Face token is set on the server, an optional AI layer adds a conversational second opinion on top. With it switched off, everything else works exactly the same.

Where do the plant and flower details come from?

From a curated dataset built specifically for Pakistani growing conditions — 83 full care sheets and a 273-flower atlas — cross-checked against Open-Meteo climate records, Wikipedia, GBIF and iNaturalist. Where a plant is fussy, the care sheet says so instead of pretending it's easy.

Is there an Urdu version?

The recommendation wizard switches between English and اردو. The rest of the site is English with Roman Urdu sprinkled in — because "apna shehar, apna mausam" says it better anyway.
